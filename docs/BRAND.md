## 1. Where this comes from

### The core value, in Vick's words

> "Fun creative expression and exploration, this is my core value as a designer, to always engage my work with methodical detailed research that opens paths for perspectives and creativity, not out the box or in I would use the box."

### What Vick told us about the brand

| Question                          | Answer                                                                       |
| --------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Who does the copy speak to first? | Studios and hiring leads                                                     |
| What kind of humour?              | Nerdy and curious: jokes that come from loving the detail                    |
| How present is faith?             | A light touch: in About and the personal parts, not in headlines or taglines |
| What should a visitor do next?    | Open to all of it: a job, a project, a chat                                  |

### What the sentence is really saying

It has three moves, and the order matters.

1. **Fun is the point.** Expression and exploration are the value, not a perk.
2. **Research is how the fun is earned.** The work is methodical and detailed first. Research is what "opens paths".
3. **The box is material.** Vick doesn't escape the constraint or obey it. He picks it up and builds with it.

Every piece of copy should carry at least one of these three moves.

### Brand nouns (the things the brand talks about)

- **The box**: the brief, the limit, the cardboard. Always something to build with.

- **The instrument**: Vick, and the site. Something you play in different ways, not a single-purpose tool.
- **The workbench**: where things get made. The desk, the mat, the tape.
- **The path**: what research opens. A route in, a different angle.
- **The forever student**: Vick is always learning, and says so.
- **The sketch**: where everything starts. Pencil before pixels.

play, tinker, explore, research, map, rework, build, test, use

- "I use the box."
- "Research like a nerd. Play like a kid."
- "Homework first. Then play."
- "I am creative."

### Words and phrases the brand does not use

outside the box (unless it is being turned down), passionate, pixel-perfect, ninja, guru, rockstar, synergy, storyteller, crafting experiences, user-centric, innovative, cutting-edge, seamless, elevate

## 3. Tagline

### Recommended

> **Research like a nerd. Play like a kid. Use the box.**

Why this one: it is the core statement in three beats and the same order (rigour, fun, resourcefulness). It is plain enough for a hiring lead to repeat, and it sounds like a person.

### Short form (for tight spaces, sign-offs, the notebook)

> **I use the box.**

### Alternatives considered

## 4. Voice

**Vick sounds like a curious, well-read nerd who is good company.** He explains how a thing works because he finds it interesting, and he assumes you might too.

| Do                                                                      | Don't                                                          |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Write in first person: "I", "my"                                        | Third person ("Vick is a…") except where Bleep is talking      |
| Use short, plain sentences                                              | Stack adjectives or clauses                                    |
| Be specific: name the tool, the size, the year, the client              | Say "various", "a range of", "many"                            |
| Let the joke come from the detail (D&D, tools, cardboard, being 198 cm) | Add a joke that could be pasted onto anyone's site             |
| Show the research before the result                                     | Lead with how beautiful it is                                  |
| State facts flatly, including awards                                    | Hype, or humble-brag                                           |
| Mention faith simply where it belongs (About, Life)                     | Put faith in headlines, taglines or project copy               |
| Use South African English and words where natural ("Yoh!", "takkie")    | Explain or italicise them unless the project is about the word |

### The nerd test

Before a line ships, ask: would this still be true and interesting if you removed the adjectives? If not, find the detail and say that.

### How funny?

One dry or nerdy line per card at most. Bleep is allowed more. Project descriptions lead with the work; the humour is seasoning.

### Who is reading

A studio lead or hiring manager with two minutes. They want to know: what does he do, how does he think, can he work with a team, is he any good, and would he be good to have around. Clients and other creatives should feel welcome too, so nothing should read as a job application.

## 5. Copy rules for this site

- **Headings**: three words or fewer where the layout is small (Bleep, buttons, notebook).
- **Bleep's lines**: a heading of up to three words and one or two lines of body. Bleep talks about Vick in the third person and is the only place that happens.
- **Buttons**: say what happens ("Enter", "Back", "Copy"). No "Click here".
- **Project cards**: what the problem was, what I found out, what I made. Only facts Vick has supplied. Never invent a result, a number or a client quote.
- **Numbers**: use them. "12 projects", "800 × 2000 px", "2020".
- **Punctuation**: no em dashes. Full stops over semicolons.
- **Length**: if it can lose a sentence, lose it.

## 7. Copy prompt (give this to an AI before it writes for the site)

```
You are writing copy for Vick Does Creative, the portfolio of Vickhal Roopram (Vick), a UX/UI, illustration and motion designer from South Africa. The site is a playable cardboard synth on a workbench.

CORE VALUE (Vick's words): "Fun creative expression and exploration, this is my core value as a designer, to always engage my work with methodical detailed research that opens paths for perspectives and creativity, not out the box or in I would use the box."

TAGLINE: Research like a nerd. Play like a kid. Use the box.
SHORT FORM: I use the box.

VALUES: Play, Curiosity, Rigour, Perspective, Resourcefulness, Expression, Craft.

BRAND NOUNS: the box (a constraint you build with), the homework (research), the instrument (Vick and the site), the workbench, the path (what research opens), the forever student, the sketch.

READER: a studio lead or hiring manager with two minutes, first. Clients and fellow creatives second. Keep every door open: a job, a project or a chat.

VOICE:
- First person ("I", "my"). Only Bleep, the TV tour guide, speaks about Vick in the third person.
- Nerdy and curious. The humour comes from loving the detail: tools, cardboard, D&D, counting things twice. At most one dry line per card. Bleep may have more.
- Simple words and short sentences. Easy to read aloud.
- Specific: name the tool, the size, the year, the client.
- Show the research before the result. Order: the problem, what I found, what I made.
- State awards and facts flatly. No hype and no humble-bragging.
- South African English.
- Faith gets a light touch: plain mentions in About and Life only. Never in headlines, taglines or project copy.

NEVER:
- Invent a fact, result, number, client or quote. Use only what Vick has supplied. If something is missing, ask.
- Use: outside the box (unless turning it down), passionate, pixel-perfect, ninja, guru, rockstar, synergy, storyteller, crafting experiences, user-centric, innovative, cutting-edge, seamless, elevate.
- Use em dashes.

FORMATS:
- Bleep: a heading of 3 words or fewer, and a body of 1 or 2 lines.
- Buttons: say what happens, in 1 or 2 words.
- Project cards: 2 to 4 sentences.
- Search and AI lines (page title, description, a project's one-line summary): plain and direct, no jokes. Title under 60 characters, description under 160.

CHECK BEFORE YOU FINISH: remove the adjectives. Is the line still true and still interesting? If not, find the detail and say that instead.
```

## 8. Copy for search engines, AI assistants and scouts (22 Nov 2026)

Most people meet the site first as a search result, a link preview or an AI assistant's answer, before they ever see the synth. That copy has one job: say plainly who Vick is and what's here, so a studio lead or scout clicks.
